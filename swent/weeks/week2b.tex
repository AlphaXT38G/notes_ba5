\subsection{Breaking the Dependency}

\begin{center}
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{images/repository-interface.png}
\end{center}

\begin{keybox}[title={Program against an interface, not a concrete class}]
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em, itemsep=3pt]
  \item \textbf{Before} --- the ViewModel calls \texttt{FirebaseFirestore} directly. There is nothing to substitute: production code and test code are the same code.
  \item \textbf{After} --- the ViewModel depends only on a \texttt{UserRepository} interface. \texttt{FirestoreUserRepository} implements it for production; \texttt{FakeUserRepository} implements it for tests.
\end{itemize}
\tcblower
This is what makes stubs, mocks, and fakes possible in the first place: you can only inject a test double where the code depends on an interface, not a hard-coded implementation.
\end{keybox}

\begin{center}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.3}
\begin{tabular}{@{}l p{4cm} p{4cm} p{4cm}@{}}
 & \textbf{Fake} \textit{(in-memory)} & \textbf{Emulator} \textit{(real Firebase, local)} & \textbf{Real Firebase} \textit{(cloud)} \\
\hline
\textit{speed} & milliseconds & seconds & seconds + network \\
\textit{checks} & your own logic & your queries and security rules & nothing you should be unit-testing \\
\textit{use for} & unit tests (hundreds of them) & integration tests (a handful) & never in CI \\
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\begin{rembox}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em, itemsep=3pt]
  \item \textbf{Stub} --- \texttt{grades(student)} is cut off from the real DB and replaced by a canned answer; nobody checks that the cut edge was ever used.
  \item \textbf{Mock} --- \texttt{securityOn()} fans out to \texttt{WindowMock} and \texttt{DoorMock}; the test verifies that \texttt{close()} was sent to both.
  \item \textbf{Fake} --- \texttt{LoginService} is cut off from \texttt{AccountDAO} and instead wired to \texttt{FakeAccountDAO}, a real (tiny) implementation backed by a \texttt{HashMap} instead of the DB.
\end{itemize}
\end{rembox}
